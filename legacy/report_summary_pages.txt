Abstract
Assigning class labels to multivariate time series simultaneous recordings from multiple sensors
arises in cardiac diagnostics, seizure monitoring, industrial fault detection, gesture recognition,
and traffic-flow analysis. The difficulty is not merely scale: three qualitatively distinct dependency
structures must be resolved at once. Within each channel, relevant events unfold through time.
Meaningful patterns frequently arise from the interactions between various channels rather than
from any one channel alone. As the recording progresses, these inter-channel interactions may
also change, making the underlying dynamics extremely non-stationary. Few strategies are able

reading but in the patterns that emerge across consecutive rows the periodic oscillation that
characterises a healthy heartbeat, the transient amplitude spike that precedes a mechanical

2.1 Overview
Research in time series classification has followed two broad trajectories. The first trajectory
feature-based methodsfocuses on extracting increasingly powerful representations from raw
sequences, culminating in random kernel transforms and deep convolutional architectures. The
second trajectorystructure-based methodsemphasizes explicitly modeling the relational structure
between variables or instances, culminating in graph neural network approaches. The proposed
ST-GoG framework occupies the intersection of these two trajectories.

4.1 Overview of the ST-GoG Framework
The ST-GoG framework is built around a three-tier representational hierarchy that processes
each multivariate time series at three complementary levels of abstraction. At theTemporal
Tier, fine-grained patterns are extracted from each channel by a Residual TCN with five dilated
causal convolutional blocks ( d∈ { 1, 2, 4, 8, 16}) and Squeeze-and-Excitation channel attention.
At theSpatial Tier, a multi-scale GCN with three parallel branches at radii r∈ { 1, 3, 5} and
learnable softmax fusion captures inter-channel correlations across local, medium, and global
neighbourhoods. At theRelational Tier, each instance is decomposed into temporal segments
by a hierarchical GoG module, which encodes within-segment structure via an inner GCN
