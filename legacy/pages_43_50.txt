5.10 Limitations and Future Improvements
The current implementation carries five limitations. First, the 10-segment GoG on EigenWorms
(T = 17,984) creates inner GCN graphs of roughly 1800 nodes each, making per-epoch computa-
tion prohibitive; strided pre-downsampling before the GoG module would mitigate this. Second,
on FaceDetection (N = 5890), per-instance graph construction is a bottleneck that batch-level
graph sharing could resolve. Third, chain adjacency encodes topological proximity rather than
data-driven correlations; supplementing with a lightweight cosine-similarity component would
improve performance on datasets where cross-channel relationships are class-discriminative but
not topologically ordered. Fourth, replacing GoG mean pooling with an attention-weighted
readout would allow selective emphasis on the most discriminative temporal segments. Fifth,
future evaluations should include F1-scores, per-class confusion matrices, and GFLOPs profiling
alongside top-1 accuracy.

Conclusions and Future Scope
Accuracy on benchmark test sets is not the only measure of a research contribution. A model
that achieves strong performance but requires impractical computation, or whose behaviour
depends heavily on dataset-specific hyperparameter search, is a less useful contribution than
one that generalises reliably at reasonable cost. The ST-GoG framework was evaluated on both
dimensions.
Across 22 UEA datasets, the improved ST-GoG architecture recorded the highest mean
test accuracy (0.743) and achieved the most individual-dataset wins relative to any single
comparison method, while keeping computational cost well below that of SAGoG’s full pairwise
instance graph. The advantage was most pronounced on datasets with limited training data
such as AtrialFibrillation (15 training instances) and on datasets with structured inter-channel
dependencies, including Libras, NATOPS, FingerMovements, and ArticularyWordRecognition,
where the combination of multi-scale graph reasoning and data augmentation proved most
valuable.

6.1 Summary of Contributions
This thesis presented theSpatiotemporal Graph-of-Graphs (ST-GoG) Framework
through four cumulative contributions. Thebaseline TCN–GCN–GoG modelestablished
a clean, modular pipeline using chain adjacency, confirming that temporal, spatial, and re-
lational encoders can be integrated in a single end-to-end trainable system. Theimproved
ST-GoG architecturebuilt on that foundation by adding a five-layer residual TCN with SE
attention, a multi-scale GCN operating at three radii ( r∈ { 1, 3, 5}) with learnable fusion, an
adaptive segment-based hierarchical GoG, and a four-pathway learnable softmax fusion head a
combination that yields the best average accuracy and ten individual dataset wins across the
22-dataset benchmark. Theadvanced training packageMixUp ( α = 0.2), stochastic depth
with exponential decay, cosine annealing with warm restarts, label smoothing, and seven-pass
TTA delivered the largest gains on small and imbalanced datasets where the risk of overfitting is
greatest. Finally, thecomprehensive empirical studyproduced per-dataset accuracy tables,
win/tie/loss tallies, average rank scores, and a Wilcoxon-validated critical-difference diagram,
providing a thorough and statistically grounded comparison against SAGoG, ROCKET, and the
baseline.
6.2 Key Findings
Several consistent empirical patterns emerged from the 22-dataset evaluation. The multi-scale
GCN operating at three radii with learnable fusion outperformed the single-radius baseline
on datasets with multi-scale temporal dependencies, confirming that spatial modelling at a
single fixed scale leaves discriminative inter-channel signals unexploited. SE attention provided
consistent but modest gains, most pronounced on datasets where a small subset of temporal
feature maps carries disproportionate class-discriminative information. Inspection of learned
pathway weights revealed that GoG-augmented streams collectively captured the majority
of fusion weight on most datasets, validating the design decision of hierarchical segment-level
reasoning over simple global pooling. Despite these advantages, ROCKET remained the strongest
competitor on datasets with strong periodic structure or very long sequences, where its random-
kernel diversity efficiently extracts discriminative statistics without the computational overhead
of graph construction.

A.1 Dataset Descriptions by Domain
The UEA Multivariate Time Series Archive [10] contains datasets from diverse domains. The
Motion and Activity Recognitiongroup (AWR, HW, LI, NA, RS, SWJ, UGL) comprises
multi-sensor inertial and position recordings in which discriminative patterns reside in the phase
and relative dynamics between channels. TheMedical and Neurologicalgroup (AF, EP,
HB, SCP1, SCP2, HMD, FM, FD, MI) covers electrocardiographic and electroencephalographic
recordings characterised by small training cohorts and clinically meaningful inter-class distinctions.
TheSensor Networks and Trafficgroup is represented principally by PEMS-SF, which
aggregates readings from 267 road-traffic-flow sensors over 144 time steps and exhibits strong
daily periodicity. TheAstronomy and Othergroup includes LSST, whose six-channel
photometric light curves capture stellar variability, as well as DuckDuckGeese, which encodes
bird wing-motion dynamics, and EigenWorms, which represents nematode body-shape evolution
via six eigenworm coefficients sampled at 17,984 time steps.
